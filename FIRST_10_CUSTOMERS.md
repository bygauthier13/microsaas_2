# First 10 Customers — the playbook

**Goal:** 10 paying firms in 90 days, about €4–5k MRR, using founder-led sales.

**Unfair advantage:**
- A hard legal date (10 July 2027).
- Monaco fines already published (€50k for a yacht brokerage, Nov 2025).
- A live demo that shows a broker's own deal shape (BVI SPV → trust → Monaco UBO) in under two minutes.

---

## 1. ICP: who buys first

**Tier A (start here): Monaco and Côte d'Azur superyacht brokerages**

| Criterion | Value |
|---|---|
| Location | Monaco, Antibes, Cannes, Nice, Saint-Tropez |
| Size | 3–40 people |
| Activity | Sales brokerage of yachts above 30m (above €7.5m) and/or charter management |
| Why now | Monaco's AMSF is inspecting and fining, with 700+ compliance notices in 2025. France's Loi 2025-532 covers yacht brokers including **charter**. Monaco is on the EU high-risk list, so their EU counterparties ask for enhanced due diligence. |
| Buyer | Managing partner or head of sales. The champion is often the office manager or MLRO who currently "does the KYC". |
| Budget proof | They already pay consultants for AML manuals and outsourced compliance officers. |
| Pain trigger | An AMSF notice, a bank questionnaire, a buyer's lawyer sending 40 PDFs by email, or a deal delayed at closing. |

**Tier B: EU yacht brokers and yards**
- Palma, Barcelona, Genoa, Viareggio, La Spezia, Amsterdam, Hamburg, Athens and Split.
- Sell this ahead of the July 2027 deadline.

**Tier C: private-jet dealers in the EU, and hypercar or collector-car dealers selling cars above €250k**
- For example in Germany, Italy, France, Benelux and Switzerland (the Swiss angle is about EU clients).

**Disqualify:**
- Brokers who only sell boats under €1m.
- US-only brokerages with no EU or Monaco office.
- Firms with an in-house AML team already on an enterprise suite (large yards). These are later Fleet-plan prospects.

## 5. Demo strategy: "build your file, live" (15 minutes)

1. **Minutes 0–2: their reality.** "What does the buyer's side send you today, and how?" Listen for: email, WhatsApp, Dropbox, lawyer delays, the bank asking questions.
2. **Minutes 2–6: their deal.** Open a deal file with *their* current boat (name, length, price). Show the Article 74 banner appearing live.
3. **Minutes 6–10: the client link.** Create the link, open it on their phone, then show the demo workspace's submitted Aurelia file. Point out three things:
   - the ownership chain;
   - Monaco and BVI flagged with their legal references;
   - the false-positive match cleared with a written rationale.
4. **Minutes 10–13: the file.** Export the AML file and scroll to the screening log: "This 'no match' line is what the AMSF asks for, and what most firms can't produce."
5. **Minutes 13–15: close.**
   - "Start the trial on this deal today; I'll check the file with you on Friday."
   - Send the signup link with their plan preselected: `/signup?plan=brokerage`.

**Rule:** never demo without one of their real deals in the room. The activation event is *their* first client link being sent.

## 8. Conversion strategy (trial to paid)

- **Day 0:** signup. Onboarding pushes them to the first deal file and the client link (the activation metric).
- **Day 1:** personal email. "Did the buyer's lawyer open the link? If not, I'll nudge them with you."
- **Day 3:** check *Founder metrics* (`/app/admin`). Who has `kyc_link_sent` but no `kyc_submitted`? Call them.
- **Day 7:** 15-minute review of their first exported file. Point out what an inspector would ask.
- **Day 11:** the automated trial-ending email fires at 3 days and again at 1 day. Call personally with the founding-firm code.
- **Day 14:** the workspace becomes read-only (files are kept). Most conversions happen when they open the second deal.

**Leading indicators to watch weekly:**
- demo → signup %
- signup → first deal %
- first deal → link sent %
- link sent → client submitted %
- trial → paid %
